Suggested structure

Eight slides that tell the whole story

  1. Problem: who needs this data, for which decisions, how fresh
  2. Sources: types, volumes, refresh, the nasty surprises you found
  3. Architecture: one diagram, tools at each layer, and why
  4. Model: facts and dimensions, with the grain in one sentence each
  1. Trust: tests and checks, the real problem one of them caught
  2. Operations + governance: schedule, re-runs, personal data
  3. Live demo: run it, re-run it, query the result
  4. Lessons and next steps: what breaks first at 100× the data
Scoring guide

What each criterion looks like at its best

CriterionWeightStrong evidence
Functional end-to-end pipeline30%Two or more source types to analytics-ready tables with one command, from a clean clone, live
Architecture & data modeling25%Layers with clear jobs; grain stated and tested; tool choices justified by data size, freshness and team
Data quality & testing20%8+ data tests with severities; one caught a real issue; freshness or volume checked
Orchestration & governance15%Idempotent incremental loads shown live; a schedule; personal data classified and protected
Presentation & reproducibility10%Clear story, on time; README lets a stranger rebuild everything
Technical Q&A

Questions you should be ready for

  • What's the grain of this fact table? Show me the test that proves it.
  • What happens if tonight's load runs twice? Or fails halfway?
  • A source renames a column tomorrow. Where does it break, and who finds out?
  • How do you handle late or duplicated records?
  • Which columns hold personal data? Who can see them raw?
  • A customer asks to be erased. Walk me through every place their data lives.
  • What would you change at 100× today's volume? Would you need Spark?
  • Why this tool and not the simpler alternative?
Great answers point to a query, a test, a run in the orchestrator or a line in the README. "We didn't handle that, and here's how we would" is a fine answer too.

Patterns across projects

What worked

  • Writing the grain down first, and testing it
  • Showing a re-run that changes nothing
  • Tests with severities and owners, not just "it passed"
  • Keeping it simple: DuckDB + dbt until the data said otherwise

What to watch

  • Append-only loads that duplicate on retry
  • Fan-out joins inflating revenue
  • Personal data copied into every layer "just in case"
  • Demos that only work on the presenter's laptop
