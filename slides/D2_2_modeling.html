Combine facts at a shared grain: lines first, join second.
Step 1: group the lines by order_id: items_amount = 4.74 + 75.76 = 80.50. The order side is already one row per order. Now a join cannot repeat anything: one row meets one row. Dimension: a table of descriptions to filter and group by. Star schema: the fact in the middle, one join to each dimension.
The star from the earlier slide returns, now with what the wide row taught. Each fact row holds four keys and its measures; each description is stored once. The keys are surrogate keys the warehouse assigns (Lab 2.2 numbers them in source-ID order; Khalid, customer 107371, is key 7214); the source IDs stay in the dimension. The date key is the Riyadh date of the order: the app wrote 27 September 22:35 UTC, which is 28 September 01:35 in Riyadh, a Monday. dim_date also flags weekends and National Day (23 September). Why a table, when date functions could work these out? Because the calendar is a business decision, written down once like the revenue definition. Qafila’s weekend is Friday and Saturday (dayofweek IN (5, 6) in DuckDB, where Sunday is 0); an analyst who types the familiar IN (0, 6) counts Sunday, a working day, and the query runs without an error. Some dates can’t be worked out at all: Ramadan and the two Eids follow the Hijri calendar and move every year, so they have to be listed in the table. And a LEFT JOIN from dim_date keeps the days a store sold nothing, which questions about average daily revenue need. dim_customer keeps city, district and signup date, and no direct identifiers: analytics doesn’t need Khalid’s name or mobile number.

11 / The words

Ten words for modeling

Every one appeared on a slide today. dbt and the labs use them all.

  • Grain · what one row stands for
  • Fact table · one row per event, with its measures
  • Measure · a number you add up
  • Dimension · descriptions to filter and group by
  • Star schema · a fact with one join to each dimension
  • Fan-out · a join that repeats a coarser number
  • Surrogate key · the warehouse’s own ID for a row
  • Unknown member · the dimension row for “no match”
  • Snapshot · a level or a process, restated per row
  • Reconcile · prove rows and money match the source
Grain first: most of the other words follow from it.

12 / Recall

Your turn: answer before you look

Five questions on Khalid’s order. Answer out loud, or with a neighbour; each key press shows an answer.

  1. Finish the sentence for fct_order_items: one row per ___. How do you test it?
    One order line. Count rows and distinct (order_id, line_no): 172,545 each.
  2. Khalid pays a 9 SAR delivery fee. Which table holds it?
    fct_orders. The fee happens once per order, so it lives at order grain.
  3. Someone joins orders onto lines and adds each line’s fee. What does Khalid’s order give?
    98.50, not 89.50: the fee is counted twice. Add the lines up first, then join.
  4. Order 5000037’s customer was erased. Which customer_key do its lines get?
    −1, the Unknown member (LEFT JOIN + COALESCE). No sale is dropped.
  5. Coffee stock reads 40, 34, 30 on three days. Can you add them up?
    Not across days: a level adds across stores on one day. On the last day the stock is 30.
